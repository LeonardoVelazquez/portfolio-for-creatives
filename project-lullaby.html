Lullaby

For two years I’ve designed the posters that introduce Lullaby YVR’s next night out. The music and lineups change every week. Making two or three posters a week taught me when to keep experimenting and when a composition is ready.

My role

Posters, copy, Reels, post scheduling and community messages. I also collaborate with other collectives and occasionally coordinate national and international bookings.

A week at Lullaby

An event announcement starts with a lineup, but the poster is only one part of getting that lineup in front of people. Over two years with Lullaby YVR, I’ve designed two or three posters a week while also writing copy, producing Reels, scheduling posts and responding to messages. Working on both sides of the announcement lets me see which details people look for once the artwork is out in the world.

Different nights, different invitations

Paranormal and Haunted Halloween both work with horror imagery. OverGround and Rave en tu Idioma belong to the international booking side of the programme. Face 2 Face turns the relationship between two performers into the subject of the flyer. Together these campaigns show how much the tone can change within one venue’s calendar.

What repetition changed

My role occasionally extends to national and international bookings and collaborating with collectives such as Vantek and Cinemagic. Balancing that work with university and DJing made time a real design constraint. With another poster due in a few days, I learned to commit to an idea, finish it and carry one lesson into the next brief.
