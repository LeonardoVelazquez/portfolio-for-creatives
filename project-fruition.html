Fruition Movement

For queer Vancouverites who feel out of place at the gym, even walking through the door can be a hurdle. Our team imagined a fitness space where movement could feel social, playful and welcoming from that first visit.

My contribution

Website design, mural illustration and merchandise. I also developed the brand guide with the team.

Project duration

14 weeks · Digital Thinking

  • →

Designing the first visit

Our team interviewed 14 people about fitness and belonging. We used those conversations to shape a gym concept for queer and gender-diverse people in Vancouver, especially beginners. For my part of the project, I focused on a moment that happens before anyone touches a piece of equipment. Someone opens the website, looks around and decides whether they can picture themselves there.

A website that lets you look around

I designed three pages around questions a first-time visitor might bring. The homepage introduces Fruition. About explains who the gym is for and why we created it. Amenities shows what visitors can actually do there, so they can explore the space at their own pace.

From a screen to a dumbbell

We built the brand guide together. My equipment and merchandise designs gave us a way to see how the identity worked on objects people would hold or move. A condom wrapper had room for a small mark. A dumbbell offered a narrow surface, while a bounce ball let the graphics wrap around a curve.

Taking the mural outside

I drew the mural as a flat illustration. Placing it on Cameron’s exterior model let us see the artwork from a visitor’s point of view. From the sidewalk, the mural would be their first encounter with Fruition, even before they reached the front door.
